I'm Guang Shi (时光), a Research Associate in the Department of Chemistry at The University of Texas at Austin. I received my Ph.D. in biophysics from the University of Maryland, College Park, and have done postdoc studies at UT Austin and the University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign.

## Research

I combine polymer physics, statistical mechanics, and data-driven inference to understand how molecular information shapes the structure, dynamics, and material properties of chromosomes and biomolecular condensates.

**The 4D genome.** I developed HIPPS-DIMES, a maximum-entropy framework that reconstructs ensembles of three-dimensional chromosome structures from Hi-C or imaging data. I use the inferred interactions to connect chromosome organization with chromatin motion and local mechanical response, and collaborate on models linking simultaneous enhancer–promoter contacts to gene expression.

**Biomolecular condensates.** My polymer theories connect molecular sequence and interactions to phase behavior, internal organization, and fluidity. They describe how local clustering and fluctuating domains influence molecular diffusion and viscosity, providing a physical route from sequence to material properties.

**Generative genome modeling (ongoing).** I am developing models that generate chromosome ensembles from epigenomic and sequence features. In parallel, I am developing methods that combine population-level structural information with sparse single-cell contacts to infer individual cells' chromatin organization.

My broader interests also include molecular motors.
